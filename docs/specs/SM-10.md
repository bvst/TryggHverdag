### Readings this spec makes

Each is stated so the reviewers can check it, not assumed quietly.

1. **"Removed"** is a responder's row taken off an unended journey by the
   removal. In M3 the walker does it during a journey (the abusive-member
   threat) and the admin does it for a member (the remove-a-member rule).
   Here the removal module does it, with no route. SM-02's "leaves" would use
   the same operation; whether a responder may leave a journey on their own
   is in no story (M3).
2. **"During a journey"** is `ACTIVE` or `LOST_CONTACT`. A removal for an
   `ENDED` journey is ignored and logged without location (SM-07), and
   changes nothing.
3. **"The responder who acknowledged an alert"** is `acknowledged_by` of the
   journey's one unresolved alert (D-114). A `RESOLVED` alert is over:
   removing its acknowledger changes nothing on it, and who helped stays on
   record.
4. **"Goes back to unacknowledged"**: `acknowledged_by` and `acknowledged_at`
   are cleared together, and the state is `OPEN`. On Q1 (a), the escalation
   time is cleared too and the alert's round goes up by one (approach item 4).
5. **"Escalation resumes"** is the escalation to SMS (LOST-07). An alert never
   escalated gets its SMS at two minutes from its opening, at once if those
   have passed: the rule LOST-07 built, unchanged. An alert already escalated
   is Q1. No push is sent again: in this plan, escalation is the SMS.
6. **"The last responder"**: the removal leaves the unended journey with no
   responder row.
7. **"The walker is warned at once"**: one message of a new kind,
   `NO_RESPONDER`, to the walker. It is non-critical (D-087) and content-free
   (D-086), written in the removal's transaction (AR-05) and due at its
   `now()`, so the push loop hands it over at its next run (every 10 s). The
   loud journey screen is M3's (D-087).
8. **The roadmap's "at L6"**: L6 runs flows "through the real API". No route
   removes a responder, so L6 drives the removal through its module, as it
   drives the watchdog through its module. Everything else in those flows
   goes through the API: the start, the heartbeats and "I'm on it".
9. **A removed responder's messages:** Q2. **A journey with no responder that
   goes silent:** Q3. **Deleting the row or marking it removed:** Q4.

## Approach (technical choices delegated to Claude, D-031)

1. **Where the code goes, and why there.**
   - **The rules:** `domain/journey.ts` gains a journey event, `remove`, with
     its rule; an alert event, `acknowledger_removed`, with its rule; a kind,
     `NO_RESPONDER`; and three lists. AR-04 keeps every transition in one
     module. The file is owned, in the safety filter and mutated (`domain`).
   - **The module:** `modules/journeys/removal.ts` (new), because a removal
     changes who follows a journey. The folder is owned, in the ai-review
     `safety` filter and in `SAFETY_PATHS`, so `ai-review.yml` is not edited
     (D-075). It is mutated in the `journeys` group (Mutation, below).
     `modules/alerts/` would have been the other place; its group is already
     the slowest (6:15 on CI, D-117).
   - **The SQL:** `adapters/journeys.ts`. The removal's read and its
     transaction; every outbox insert writes its alert's round; on Q3 (a),
     the open and the escalation meet a journey with no responder, and the
     unheard-alert count. Owned and filtered; proven at L3 (D-095).
   - **On Q3 (a):** `modules/alerts/sms-check.ts` also counts unheard alerts.
   - **Unchanged:** `api.ts`, `api-process.ts`, `http.ts`, the contracts,
     `worker.ts`, `bin/worker.ts`, `modules/alerts/watchdog.ts`,
     `escalation.ts`, `outbox.ts`, `acknowledgement.ts`,
     `modules/journeys/service.ts`, `adapters/db.ts`, `adapters/healthchecks.ts`,
     `config.ts`, Terraform and the workflows. `worker.ts` already hands the
     SMS check the database store, which gains the new count, so its wiring
     does not change.
   - **No production caller in M2.** Nothing in `api.ts` or `worker.ts`
     constructs the removal module: only tests do, until M3's route (D-115's
     scope). Stated so nobody reads its absence from `api.ts` as an oversight.

2. **The removal rule** (`transition`, a sixth journey event, AR-04). Its
   situation is `JourneyForRemoval { id, state, responderIds }`, and its event
   is `RemoveEvent { type: 'remove', responderId }`. In its order:
   1. no journey → refused, `JOURNEY_NOT_FOUND`;
   2. `ENDED` → ignored, `JOURNEY_ENDED` (SM-07);
   3. `responderId` not among `responderIds` → unchanged, `NOT_A_RESPONDER`.
      The walker is never a responder, so the walker's own ID lands here. IDs
      are compared exactly: the stores hand them back in lower case;
   4. otherwise → `{ type: 'removed', state, lastResponder }`, where
      `lastResponder` is true when no other responder ID is left. The
      journey's state does not change.
   - No event ID (D-103's reading). The pair (journey, responder) makes a
     repeat harmless: the second removal finds no row and answers unchanged.
   - Pure, total over its lists, no clock.

3. **The module** (`createRemovalService({ journeys, log }).remove({
   journeyId, responderId })`), written as "I'm on it" is:
   - read the journey and its responders without a lock
     (`journeyForRemoval`), and ask the rule;
   - answer a refusal, an ignore or an unchanged from that read. An `ENDED`
     journey gets one `removal_ignored` line, naming the journey and the
     reason;
   - for `removed`, call the store, which asks the same rule again under the
     journey's row and writes what it decides. A refusal under the lock is
     answered as the read's would have been;
   - a failed read or store writes one `removal_failed` line, with the stage
     and the SQLSTATE, and throws on, so M3's route will answer 500, never a
     2xx;
   - no line for a successful removal; no user ID in any line (PRIV-07).

4. **The reset (SM-10)**, the alert rule's third event (`alertTransition`).
   Its situation is the alert's `{ id, state, acknowledgedBy }`, and its event
   is `AcknowledgerRemovedEvent { type: 'acknowledger_removed', responderId }`.
   In its order:
   1. no unresolved alert → unchanged;
   2. `RESOLVED` → unchanged: the alert is over, and who helped stays on record;
   3. `acknowledgedBy` is not this responder (someone else, or nobody) →
      unchanged;
   4. otherwise → `{ type: 'reset', state: 'OPEN' }`, whatever the state was.
      A record with this responder on it in another state (`OPEN` or
      `ESCALATED` with someone recorded, put in directly) is reset too: a
      half-done record is met in the safe direction, as D-114 met one.
   - **What the store writes for a reset** (Q1 (a)): `state = 'OPEN'`,
     `acknowledged_by = null`, `acknowledged_at = null`, `sms_raised_at =
     null`, `round = round + 1`, in one statement guarded by `state <>
     'RESOLVED' and acknowledged_by = $removed`, which must change exactly one
     row. It also withdraws the alert's unsent `WITHDRAWN_WHEN_RESET` kinds:
     its `ACKNOWLEDGED` notices, by D-113's reasoning. "Someone is on it" is
     stale once nobody is, and it is a false reassurance that could stop a
     responder from acting.
   - **Why the escalation time is cleared, rather than the state going back
     to `ESCALATED` with it kept**, which LOST-07's spec sketched:
     - On Q1 (a) the second round must escalate. The escalation rule's
       "already escalated, once per alert" reads `sms_raised_at`. Cleared,
       with the round raised, the second round's SMS is the first SMS of a
       new round, written by the escalation LOST-07 built and tested, with no
       change to its rule, its read or its write, beyond copying the round.
     - Nothing is lost. LOST-07-AC17 holds each SMS's `created_at` equal to
       `sms_raised_at` to the microsecond, so the first round's time stays on
       its round-1 SMS rows.
     - The alternative, keeping `sms_raised_at` and adding the round of the
       last SMS (`sms_round`), adds a column and changes the rule, the read
       and the write on the path that pages, for no difference in behaviour.
     - So `sms_raised_at` now means "this round's escalation". `ESCALATED`
       still means "escalated in this round, its SMS written".
   - **What escalation does after a reset:** nothing new. The alert is `OPEN`,
     nobody is recorded and it has no escalation time, so the next sweep at
     or past two minutes from `opened_at` escalates it, at once when those
     have passed, as LOST-07-AC3 already escalates such an alert.

5. **The round** (the outbox's unique key, LOST-07's recommendation).
   - `alerts.round` starts at 1 and goes up by one with each reset.
   - `outbox.round`: every alert message is written with its alert's round
     at the time of writing, in SQL, so it cannot drift. That covers the
     open's `LOST_CONTACT` (1), the notices, the SMS and the stand-downs.
   - The unique key becomes (alert, recipient, kind, round). A second
     acknowledgement's notices and a second round's SMS are each taken; a
     second message of one kind for one recipient in one round is still
     refused, which guards the races LOST-02 to LOST-07 rely on.
   - Rejected: re-arming the old row (it loses who was told what, and when);
     dropping the key (it guards the races); a notice kind per round (the
     enum would grow with each reset).

6. **The store's removal** (`removeResponder`), one transaction:
   1. `set_config('lock_timeout', LOCK_WAIT_LIMIT_MS, true)`. Every removal
      bounds its own waits, whichever pool runs it: the API's pool already
      has 5 s, and the worker's has no limit of its own (D-108);
   2. **the journey's row first** (D-112): `select id, walker_id, state from
      journeys where id = $j for update`, waiting (a person's action, as
      "I'm home" waits);
   3. **under that lock**, read the responder rows and ask the removal rule
      (AR-04). Anything but `removed` is answered `{ outcome: 'not_removed',
      decision }`, writing nothing;
   4. `delete from journey_responders where journey_id = $j and responder_id
      = $r`, which must delete exactly one row (Q4 (a));
   5. the journey's one unresolved alert, read under the lock, and the reset
      rule; on `reset`, approach item 4's update and withdrawal;
   6. on Q2 (a), withdraw every unsent, not yet withdrawn message of this
      journey's alerts addressed to the removed responder, whatever its kind
      (`WITHDRAWN_WHEN_REMOVED`, every alert kind), at `now()`. Attempts and
      the last failure are kept, as every withdrawal keeps them;
   7. when the rule said `lastResponder`, write the walker's warning
      (approach item 7), which must insert exactly one row;
   8. answer `{ outcome: 'removed', resetAlertId, messages }`, `messages` being
      the warning when one was written.
   - Lock order: the journey's row, then its responder row, then the alert's
     row, then outbox rows. That is the order the open, the escalation, the
     acknowledgement and the resolution already take. Every wait is bounded by
     the removal's own 5 s lock limit and the pools' 10 s idle limit. The
     worker's marks can wait on the removal's withdrawal, as on the API's
     (D-112, loop 1): that stalls delivery only, and is bounded the same way.
   - Not rewritten as the heartbeat's errors are. Nothing here binds a
     location or a phone number, and the module logs the SQLSTATE alone, as
     `recordHome` and `recordAcknowledgement` do.

7. **The walker's warning** (SM-02, D-087).
   - A new kind, `NO_RESPONDER`, last in `MESSAGE_KINDS` and in `PUSH_KINDS`.
     The push claim takes it, and the SMS claim never does.
   - **The outbox holds a journey's message:** `alert_id` becomes nullable,
     `journey_id` (references `journeys`) is added, and a check holds
     **exactly one** of the two (`num_nonnulls(alert_id, journey_id) = 1`). The
     check names no kind, because a value added to an enum in a migration's
     transaction cannot be used in that transaction (approach item 9).
   - The warning: `journey_id` the journey, `alert_id` null, recipient the
     journey's walker, round 1, written and due at `now()`. It is
     content-free at the port: message ID, recipient, kind (D-086).
   - A new list, `JOURNEY_MESSAGE_KINDS = ['NO_RESPONDER']`: the kinds a
     journey's messages carry, with no alert. Nothing withdraws them in M2:
     the resolution, the acknowledgement, the reset and the open all withdraw
     by alert, and the removal by recipient, which is never the walker.
   - Rejected: a table of its own for journey messages. It would need a
     second claim and sender, or a union in the one claim, so more code on
     the delivery path, and two orders instead of one.
   - It is non-critical: M3's push adapter maps the level from the kind
     (D-087).

8. **A journey with no responder** (Q3 (a)).
   - **The open** opens it as any other: the journey goes to `LOST_CONTACT`
     with an `OPEN` alert and no message. The throw goes. Its earlier
     stand-downs' withdrawal finds no recipient, and so withdraws nothing.
   - **The escalation:** `alertsDueForEscalation` reads only alerts whose
     journey has a responder row. Under the lock, `escalateInside` answers
     `skipped`, writing nothing, when the journey has none: who to text is
     the write's to know, as today's throw is. The domain's escalation rule
     does not change.
   - **The page:** `unheardAlertCount()` counts the unresolved alerts whose
     journey has no responder row, with the database's `now()`, in one

## Acceptance criteria

The journey J is started through the API by the walker W, from the device D,
naming the responders R1, R2 and R3, unless a criterion says otherwise.
"Removed" means removed through the removal module.

### Removing a responder

**SM-10-AC1 — A responder is removed from a running journey, and nothing else
changes.** *(SM-10, SM-02, SM-07, SM-08)*
- **Given** J `ACTIVE`
- **When** R2 is removed
- **Then** the answer is removed; J's responders are R1 and R3; J is still
  `ACTIVE`, with the same start, device and last contact; no alert is opened,
  and no message is written, since two responders remain
- **And** removing R2 again answers unchanged, `NOT_A_RESPONDER`, and changes
  nothing (safe twice, with no event ID: D-103's reading)
- **And** removing W, a user who follows only another journey, an ID that is
  no user's, or R1's ID in upper case answers `NOT_A_RESPONDER` and changes
  nothing
- **And** for a journey ID no journey has, the answer is refused,
  `JOURNEY_NOT_FOUND`: nothing written, no line
- **And** for an `ENDED` journey the answer is ignored, `JOURNEY_ENDED`; its
  responder rows stay, and one `removal_ignored` line names the journey and
  the reason
- **And** another journey's responders, R2's rows on other journeys, and every
  other row are unchanged.

**SM-10-AC2 — A removed responder is on the journey for nothing that
follows.** *(SM-10, LOST-02, LOST-03, LOST-06, LOST-07)*
- **Given** R2 removed while J is `ACTIVE`
- **When** D goes silent, and the loops run past five minutes and past two
  minutes after the alert opened, and contact then comes back
- **Then** the lost-contact push, the escalation SMS and the stand-down are
  each written for and handed to R1 and R3 only, never R2
- **And** R2's "I'm on it" for J's alert is 404 `ALERT_NOT_FOUND`, with the
  body a stranger gets, and writes nothing
- **And** R1's "I'm on it" writes its notice for R3 only.

**SM-10-AC3 — The removal decides under the journey's row.** *(SM-10, SM-09)*
- **Given** a real PostgreSQL, and J's row held by another transaction
- **When** a removal of R2 arrives
- **Then** it waits for the row (`pg_stat_activity`), then decides by what the
  holder left: removed; unchanged if the holder removed R2; ignored if the
  holder ended J
- **And** with `RACERS` removals of R2 at once on separate connections, for
  `RACE_ROUNDS` rounds, exactly one answers removed and every other
  unchanged, none an error, with one reset at most and one warning at most.

### The resumed-escalation rule

**SM-10-AC4 — Removing the responder who acknowledged an alert puts it back
to unacknowledged.** *(SM-10, LOST-06)*
- **Given** J `LOST_CONTACT`, its alert A acknowledged by R1 through the API
  60 s after it opened, with `ACKNOWLEDGED` notices for R2 (sent) and R3 (not
  yet claimed)
- **When** R1 is removed
- **Then** A is `OPEN`; `acknowledged_by` and `acknowledged_at` are both null;
  `sms_raised_at` is null; its round is one more than before; `opened_at`,
  `silent_since` and J's state are unchanged
- **And** R3's unsent notice is withdrawn at the removal's now, keeping its
  attempts and last failure; R2's sent notice is as it was
- **And** removing R2 or R3 instead leaves A as it was: `ACKNOWLEDGED` by R1,
  its round unchanged
- **And** when A is `RESOLVED` (acknowledged by R1, then contact came back),
  removing R1 leaves A as it was, who acknowledged it included
- **And** an alert with R1 recorded in another state (`OPEN`, or `ESCALATED`,
  put in directly) is reset too.

**SM-10-AC5 — Escalation resumes for an alert never escalated: at its two
minutes, or at once if they have passed. The roadmap's "both rules at L6",
its first half.** *(SM-10, LOST-07, REL-07)*
- **Given** J, D sending a heartbeat every 60 s and then nothing, the loops
  running every 10 s on the fake clock, and A opened at five minutes with
  every responder's push accepted
- **When** R1 says "I'm on it" through the API 60 s after A opened, and R1 is
  removed 90 s after it opened
- **Then** up to 119 999 ms after the opening nothing is escalated, and the
  SMS fake holds nothing
- **And** at exactly 120 000 ms the sweep escalates A: `ESCALATED`,
  `sms_raised_at` that now, and one `LOST_CONTACT_SMS` each for R2 and R3 in
  A's current round, none for R1 or W; the SMS fake receives exactly those two
- **And** in another run, with R1 removed five minutes after the opening, the
  first sweep after the removal escalates A, within 10 s of the removal
- **And** with no removal, the acknowledgement at 60 s still means no SMS for
  ten minutes (LOST-07's second test, unchanged).

**SM-10-AC6 — Escalation resumes for an alert already escalated: every
remaining responder is texted again, at once (Q1 (a)).** *(SM-10, LOST-07,
REL-07)*
- **Given** A escalated at 120 000 ms, with round-1 SMS for R1, R2 and R3: R1's
  and R2's accepted, R3's failing `NO_TARGET`
- **When** R1 says "I'm on it" three minutes after the opening, which
  withdraws R3's unsent SMS as LOST-07 built, and R1 is removed at four minutes
- **Then** the first sweep after the removal escalates A again: `ESCALATED`,
  `sms_raised_at` that sweep's now, and one new `LOST_CONTACT_SMS` each for R2
  and R3 in the new round, each with a message ID of its own; none for R1; the
  round-1 rows as they were
- **And** the SMS fake receives one more SMS for each of R2 and R3 and none
  for R1; the push fake receives no new `LOST_CONTACT`
- **And** later sweeps escalate nothing more while nobody acknowledges.

**SM-10-AC7 — A second acknowledgement, a second reset, and the round in the
unique key.** *(SM-10, LOST-06, LOST-07)*
- **Given** AC6's alert, escalated again
- **When** R2 says "I'm on it" through the API
- **Then** 200; A is `ACKNOWLEDGED` by R2 at that now; one `ACKNOWLEDGED`
  notice is written for R3 in the current round, none for R1 or R2; R3's
  unsent SMS of the current round is withdrawn
- **When** R2 is removed too
- **Then** A is `OPEN` again, its round raised again, and the next sweep texts
  R3 alone
- **And** the database refuses a second message of one kind for the same
  alert, recipient and round, and takes one in another round; it refuses a
  round under 1 (L3).

**SM-10-AC8 — One lost-contact push per responder per alert, and one SMS per
responder per round.** *(SM-10, LOST-02, LOST-07)*
- **Then** across the open, the escalation, any number of acknowledgements and
  resets, and every later sweep, each responder has at most one
  `LOST_CONTACT` for the alert, written by the open: no reset and no
  escalation writes one (the server half of D-087's owed test)
- **And** for any sequence of acknowledgements, removals, sweeps, the passing
  of time, fresh and stale heartbeats and "I'm home" (fast-check, in the
  shared behaviour suite: the fake at L2, the adapter at L3 with fewer runs),
  after every step:
  - an unresolved alert's acknowledger, if any, is a responder of its journey;
  - an alert holds one `LOST_CONTACT_SMS` per round in which it escalated,
    for each responder its journey had when that round's SMS were written,
    and none for a round in which it did not escalate;
  - after the next sweep, every unresolved alert that is not acknowledged in
    D-114's sense, was opened two minutes ago or more, and whose journey has
    a responder, is escalated in its current round;
  - no removed responder has a message of the journey's alerts that is
    neither sent nor withdrawn (Q2 (a));
  - the walker holds one `NO_RESPONDER` for each time the journey went from
    one responder to none while unended;
  - no two messages share an alert, a recipient, a kind and a round.

**SM-10-AC9 — The removal and "I'm on it" meet on the journey's row, and an
acknowledgement from a responder removed since its read is `ALERT_NOT_FOUND`
under the lock.** *(SM-10, LOST-06, SM-09)*
- **Given** a real PostgreSQL, and A unacknowledged
- **When** R1's acknowledgement has read A, with R1 a responder, and then
  waits for J's row while R1's removal holds it and commits
- **Then** the acknowledgement answers 404 `ALERT_NOT_FOUND` from the store's
  decision under the lock, the branch LOST-06 left untested, and writes
  nothing: A unacknowledged, no notice
- **When** R1's acknowledgement holds J's row and commits, and R1's removal
  waits for it
- **Then** the removal resets A (AC4)
- **And** when they start at the same moment on separate connections, for
  `RACE_ROUNDS` rounds, every round ends in one of the two, never with A
  acknowledged by someone who is not a responder
- **And** at L6 the fake's `beforeNext` gives both orders, and the 404 comes
  through the API.

**SM-10-AC10 — The removal meets the open, the escalation and the resolution
on the journey's row.** *(SM-10, LOST-02, LOST-03, LOST-07, SM-04)*
- **Given** each pair in both orders: forced at L3 by transactions held open,
  and given at L2 and L6 by the fake's hooks
- **Then** a removal before the open: the open writes for the remaining
  responders only. After it: the removed responder's unsent `LOST_CONTACT` is
  withdrawn
- **And** a removal before the escalation: the SMS go to the remaining
  responders only. After it: the removed responder's unsent SMS is withdrawn
- **And** a removal before contact comes back or "I'm home": the stand-downs
  go to the remaining responders only. After contact comes back: the removed
  responder's unsent stand-down is withdrawn. After "I'm home": J is `ENDED`,
  so the removal is ignored, `JOURNEY_ENDED` (AC1, SM-07), and the stand-down
  goes as written. *Corrected 2026-10-09 in the red phase:* this criterion
  first said the stand-down was withdrawn after "I'm home" too, which AC1
  rules out (`test-author`)
- **And** once the removal has committed, the removed responder never has a
  message of J's alerts that is neither sent nor withdrawn.

### What a removed responder receives

**SM-10-AC11 — A removed responder hears nothing more (Q2 (a)).** *(SM-10,
LOST-03)*
- **Given** J `LOST_CONTACT`, A `ESCALATED`, and R2 holding: a `LOST_CONTACT`

  `ACKNOWLEDGED` notice never claimed; and a `HOME` stand-down of an earlier

  the SMS port and not yet answered (the fake's `holdAnswers`)
- **When** R2 is removed
- **Then** each of R2's unsent messages of J's alerts is withdrawn at the
  removal's now, whatever its kind, keeping attempts and last failure, and is
  never handed to a port again; the accepted one is as it was
- **And** the SMS in the port's hands finishes, is marked as the port answers,
  and is never retried
- **And** every other responder's messages are as they were
- **And** when A then resolves, R2 gets no stand-down, and R1 and R3 get one
  each.

- **Then** nothing is written for W
- **When** R2 is removed
- **Then** exactly one `NO_RESPONDER` is written for W, naming J and no alert,

  UUID equal to no user's, journey's, alert's or device's ID; nothing is
  written for anyone else; J is still `ACTIVE`
- **And** the same while J is `LOST_CONTACT`, its alert open
- **And** no warning when a removal leaves a responder, for an `ENDED`
  journey, or for a removal answered unchanged
- **And** the SMS claim never hands it out, and the SMS check never counts it
  as an SMS.

**SM-10-AC13 — One warning for the last responder, whoever removes.** *(SM-10,
SM-02)*
- **When** J's last two responders are removed at the same moment, on separate

**SM-10-AC14 — The warning is a journey's message, not an alert's.** *(SM-10,
SM-02)*
- **Then** the database refuses an outbox row naming both an alert and a

- **And** a `NO_RESPONDER` is withdrawn by nothing: not a resolution, an

## Risks and failure modes

- **[F6](../plan/03-safety-reliability-security.md#failure-modes), the
  missed alert.** This task is F6's case of "someone is on it, and then
  nobody is". Its dangers, and what holds each:
  - **The others stood back.** They were told someone is on it (D-113), and
    a delivered notice cannot be recalled. The resumed escalation's SMS is
    what tells them nobody is now: at once on Q1 (a), which is why Q1
    recommends it. An unsent notice is withdrawn at the reset (AC4).
  - **A stale notice overtaking.** A notice withdrawn by the reset but already
    in the push port's hands could reach a responder after a later
    stand-down: the resolution's hold sees only what it withdraws, as
    LOST-07 accepted for an SMS the acknowledgement withdrew. Past the port,
    APNs and FCM promise no order, and the app shows the alert's current
    state (M3).
  - **A half reset.** The reset is one statement that clears both halves of
    the acknowledgement together (the check takes both null), and the
    escalation rule still sends the SMS for any missing half (D-114, D-116).
  - **The SMS of the second round failing.** The SMS check pages after 60 s,
    as for the first round (LOST-07).
  - **The page by email, at night.** As LOST-07 said: D-079's go-live question.
- **[F7](../plan/03-safety-reliability-security.md#failure-modes), the
  watchdog.** Today a journey with no responder that goes silent fails every
  sweep and holds the worker's check down, which hides any later watchdog
  failure until the journey ends. On Q3 (a) the sweep stays healthy (AC15).
  The masking moves, smaller, to the SMS check: while one alert is unheard,
  the check stays failing, so a later SMS failure adds no new page, though
  each minute's lines still say which. It is bounded by the alert's end
  (contact back, "I'm home", or task 8's 24-hour rule). Q3 (b) removes it, at
  the cost of a third check.
- **[F2](../plan/03-safety-reliability-security.md#failure-modes), false
  alarms and cost.** A second round costs one SMS per remaining responder
  (about €0.05 each, inside D-024's range), and needs the acknowledger removed
  during an alert: rare.
- **[F8](../plan/03-safety-reliability-security.md#failure-modes), a bad
  release.** The L6 flows run on every pull request under the `journeys`
  mutation group, and L3 in CI.
  - **The migration** swaps a unique constraint, relaxes `alert_id` and adds
    an enum value. It takes brief exclusive locks on `alerts` and `outbox`
    while the old worker runs: milliseconds at the private group's scale. If
    PostgreSQL refused any of it, the pre-run hook would fail and
    `deploy-staging` would go red, with the old version still running: loud.
  - **The deploy overlap.** The old worker's push claim lists no
    `NO_RESPONDER`, so a warning written in the overlap waits for the new
    worker: delayed, not lost. An old process writes every message in round
    1 (the default). In M2 nothing resets an alert in production, so this
    cannot meet a second round. From M3, an old API's acknowledgement in a
    second round could meet the unique key and roll back to a 500, which the
    app retries after the deploy: loud, and harmless. LOST-03's deploy-overlap
    item already asks M3 to close such windows.
- **[F10](../plan/03-safety-reliability-security.md#failure-modes) and the
  abusive-member threat.** Once M3 has a route, someone holding the walker's
  unlocked phone could remove every responder, which silences every alert:
  "the ability … to silence alerts" is what the threat model protects. The
  walker is warned (AC12), and on Q3 (a) the owner is paged if that journey
  then goes silent. The route's access rules are M3's to design (Left for
  later).
- **[F1](../plan/03-safety-reliability-security.md#failure-modes),

### Q1 — When the acknowledger of an alert that has already sent its SMS is removed, does everyone get a second SMS, and when?

**Why it is asked.** SM-10 says "escalation resumes". For an alert that never
reached its SMS, that is settled: the SMS goes at two minutes from the
alert's opening, at once if those have passed. For an alert that already
sent its SMS, someone then tapped "I'm on it" and was removed. The others
were told by push that someone is on it, and they may have stood back. Who
gets texted again, and when, is a safety and cost choice.

**Options:**
- **(a) At once.** At the removal the alert goes back to `OPEN`, with no
  escalation time and its round raised. The next sweep, within about 10 s,
  texts every remaining responder again, because its two minutes have long
  passed. No push is sent again.
- **(b) After a fresh two minutes**, unless someone acknowledges meanwhile.
  This needs a time to count from (a column for when the round started), and
  the escalation's rule, its read and its stuck check count from it. Read
  strictly, it would also delay an alert that never escalated: one
  acknowledged at 30 s and reset at 1:50 would escalate at 3:50, not 2:00.
- **(c) No second SMS.** The alert goes back to `ESCALATED`, keeping its
  escalation time, and nothing more is sent. The remaining responders' last
  word may be "someone is on it".

**Recommendation: (a).**
- The others were told someone was on it, and nobody is now. The SMS is the
  only thing that tells them.
- The alert is at least two minutes old, so seven or more minutes after
  last contact. Waiting another two minutes adds delay at the worst moment,
  and nothing prompts anyone to acknowledge meanwhile.
- It costs one SMS per remaining responder (about €0.05 each, D-024), and
  needs an acknowledger removed during an alert, which is rare.
- It reuses the escalation LOST-07 built, unchanged (approach item 4).

**If answered otherwise:**
- **(b):** `alerts` gains a round-start time; the escalation rule, its read,
  `escalateInside`'s guard and the stuck check count from it; AC5's
  "at exactly 120 000 ms" becomes two minutes after the reset, and AC6's "at
  once" becomes "two minutes after the removal"; `domain`'s and `alerts`'
  mutants grow.
- **(c):** the reset keeps `sms_raised_at` and goes to `ESCALATED` when it is
  set, else `OPEN` (LOST-07's sketch); AC6 becomes "no second SMS"; the round
  then serves only the second acknowledgement's notices.

### Q2 — What does a removed responder still receive?

**Why it is asked.** When a responder is removed during an alert, they may
have been told of the loss, and some of their messages may not have gone out
yet. D-111 says every responder told of a loss is stood down, because one
never told it is over "may still be calling 112 or out looking". The
remove-a-member rule says a removed member "immediately stops … receiving
alerts", and the abusive-member threat is the reason the walker can remove
anyone. That is privacy against safety.

**Options:**
- **(a) Nothing more.** At the removal, every unsent message of the journey's
  alerts to them is withdrawn, whatever its kind: a lost-contact push, an
  SMS, a notice, an earlier alert's stand-down. Nothing later is written to
  them, so there is no stand-down either. One already in a provider's hands
  cannot be recalled.
- **(b) No more alerts, but a stand-down.** Their unsent alerts are
  withdrawn, but when the alert resolves they still get the stand-down if
  they were written a lost-contact message for it. The resolution would then
  write to someone no longer on the journey.
- **(c) Nothing withdrawn.** Their unsent messages go out as written; no
  stand-down later, because they have no row.

**Recommendation: (a).**
- It is what the remove-a-member rule asks for, "immediately".
- A stand-down ("back in contact", "is home") tells a removed person the
  walker's state, which the responders-only rule keeps from anyone not on the
  journey, and the abusive-member threat is about exactly that person.

### Q3 — What happens when a journey left with no responder goes silent?

**Why it is asked.** SM-02 lets a journey run on with no responder, once the
walker is warned. If that journey then goes silent, nobody can be told.
Today the watchdog refuses to open the alert, so the sweep fails every 10 s
and the worker's check pages you. The check then stays down until the
journey ends, which hides any other watchdog failure meanwhile, and the
journey stays `ACTIVE` in the record while it is silent. The same happens to
an open alert whose last responder is removed, at its SMS. Who, if anyone,
learns of it is a safety choice.

**Options:**
- **(a) The alert opens with nobody to tell, and you are paged through the
  SMS check.** The journey goes to `LOST_CONTACT`, the sweep stays healthy,
  and each minute the `staging-sms` check reports failing while such an alert
  is unresolved, with a line saying how many. It ends when contact comes
  back, "I'm home", or task 8's 24-hour rule resolves it. Nothing for you to
  set up. While it lasts, the check is already failing, so a later failed SMS
  adds no new page.
- **(b) As (a), with a check of its own** (a third Healthchecks.io check), so
  nothing overlaps. You create the check and a secret, as A-32 did, and plan
  and apply after the merge, as A-33. Terraform, the worker's wiring and its
  tests grow.
- **(c) The alert opens with nobody to tell, one log line, and no page.** The
  walker was warned when the last responder went.
- **(d) As today:** refused, the sweep fails, and the worker's check pages and
  stays down until the journey ends.

**Recommendation: (a).**
- You are the only person left who can do anything. A page is loud, and
  silence is the failure this app exists to prevent.
- It frees the worker's check to say only whether the watchdog works, which
  (d) does not.
- It costs you nothing to set up. The case is rare: a removal of the last
  responder, then a silence.
- (b) is the cleaner page; it can be added later if the overlap ever matters.
- Your page arrives by email (A-08), until D-079's go-live question.

### Q4 — When a responder is removed, is their place on the journey deleted, or kept and marked?

**Why it is asked.** Removing a responder can delete their row in
`journey_responders`, or keep it, marked with when they were removed. One keeps
less about people; the other keeps a record of who followed whom, and until
when. How long journey records are kept is already M4's open question. This
is privacy, with a safety edge.

**Options:**
- **(a) Delete the row.** Every path that tells a responder (the open, the
  escalation, the stand-downs, "I'm on it" and its notices) already reads
  these rows, so a removed responder is left out of all of them by
  construction. Afterwards, nothing says they followed and were removed,
  beyond messages they were already sent.
- **(b) Keep it, marked removed.** A record of who followed the journey and
  when they stopped. Every one of those paths must then leave marked rows out,
  and one that forgets keeps alerting a removed, possibly abusive, member.

**Recommendation: (a).** Privacy by construction (AR-11), less data kept, and
the remove-a-member rule's "immediately" holds for every path, the ones M3
adds included, without each having to remember. The record (b) would keep is
the kind M4's retention question is about; (a) does not prejudge it beyond
this one link.

**If answered otherwise:** `journey_responders` gains a removal time; every
statement that reads responders (five in the adapter, and the fake's) gains
"not removed"; a criterion holds that each of them leaves removed rows out; AC1
reads "marked", and AC21 gains the column.
